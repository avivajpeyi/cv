Dear members of the Appointment Committee,

I am writing to apply for the Lecturer position in Statistics. I am currently a
Research Fellow in the Department, working with Professor Renate Meyer on
Bayesian noise modelling and evidence estimation. My research sits at the
intersection of Bayesian statistics, computational methods, and
gravitational-wave science, and I am eager to contribute to the Department's
teaching, research, supervision, and service as a continuing member of its
academic community.

\block{Research, collaboration, and funding}

My research develops Bayesian and computational methods for difficult
inference problems, including parameter estimation, spectral-density
modelling, evidence estimation, variational inference, and scalable
uncertainty quantification. I have 18 short-author papers and preprints, five
of them as first author, alongside 30 large-collaboration
publications. Recent work in \emph{Physical Review D} and \emph{MNRAS} includes
Bayesian P-spline models for \acronym{lisa} noise, multivariate spectral-density
estimation, and evidence estimation through density approximation.
Although motivated by astronomy, the methods apply broadly to time
series, non-parametric modelling, Bayesian computation, and data science. At
the 2025 \acronym{aut} Mathematical Modelling and Analytics Symposium, I
demonstrated the multivariate P-spline methodology using \acronym{eeg}
recordings and financial time series alongside astrophysical data.

I am already embedded in the Statistics Department's astronomy research community and
collaborate closely with Professor Renate Meyer, Matthew Edwards, and Kate Lee.
I also bring complementary international links as the only member of the LIGO
Scientific Collaboration in New Zealand. I contribute to
\acronym{ligo--virgo--kagra} analysis infrastructure, including the
\software{parallel\_bilby} codebase, and am a current developer of the
\software{COMPAS} rapid binary-population-synthesis code. Together with my
\acronym{lisa} collaborations, these roles would support an independent and
collaborative research programme from the outset.

I would develop a programme in Bayesian, computational, and machine-learning
methods for complex, high-dimensional, and time-dependent data --- spanning
non-parametric spectral models, simulation-based and variational inference, and
neural surrogates for expensive likelihoods --- recruit and co-supervise
postgraduate students, and pursue external funding. I have secured two
successful \acronym{adacs} Merit Allocation awards for research-software
development and am Principal Investigator on a pending 2026 Dumont d'Urville
NZ--France application.

\block{Teaching}

I was a teaching assistant at Monash University for four years, across three
units. For
\coursecode{fit3170} Software Engineering Practice I independently mentored two
agile teams of roughly 20 students each year, monitored their use of the Scaled
Agile Framework, advised on technical stacks, graded their work, recruited
guest lecturers and external clients, and delivered a guest lecture. My 2021
teaching report for the course records student satisfaction of 88/100 raw and
98/100 weighted --- both in Monash's ``Very High'' band, against a Teaching
Faculty average of 82/100. The survey covered the full 97-student cohort, of
whom I taught about 40; 16 of the 17 respondents rated my teaching High or Very
High. For \coursecode{fit9136} Algorithms and Programming Foundations in
Python, a postgraduate course, I delivered tutorials, developed automated
grading, and co-authored assignments and examinations for the course lecturer's
review.

Beyond formal courses, I have designed and independently delivered training on
Bayesian parameter estimation and parallel computing for the 2023 NZ Summer
School on Gravitational Waves and the 2023 NZ Gravity Workshop at Canterbury,
and I have accepted an invitation to lecture Master's and PhD students on
Bayesian parameter estimation at the 2026 International Training Workshop on
Frontiers from Quanta to Cosmos Physics in Beijing. I have not yet been a
lecturer or course coordinator; Professor Chunyang Chen,
lecturer-in-charge of both Monash courses, can speak to the quality and
independence of my teaching.

\block{Postgraduate supervision}

At Monash University and the University of Auckland I have supported two PhD
students, three MSc students, and an undergraduate scholar alongside their
official supervisors, on variational inference, Bayesian spectral estimation,
neural surrogates, and normalising flows. This has been day-to-day research
supervision rather than a formal co-supervisor appointment; Professor Renate
Meyer can speak to it directly. I am ready to take on course coordination and
formal co-supervision from the outset.

\block{Fit with the Department's teaching}

My strongest immediate contributions to advanced Applied Statistics teaching
would be in time series, Bayesian computation, simulation, and computational
data science. In the Department, these align with
\coursecode{stats 369} Data Science Practice, \coursecode{stats 769} Advanced
Data Science Practice, \coursecode{stats 783} Simulation and Monte Carlo
Methods, and \coursecode{stats 731} Bayesian Inference. I would also
contribute to large Stage I service courses as part of a teaching team,
bringing an applied, project-led approach that connects statistical reasoning
with modern programming and real datasets.

The Department's interest in the statistical foundations of deep learning
aligns with my background in machine learning and probabilistic inference:
neural surrogates and active machine learning in research, and \acronym{nlp}
speech models and \acronym{cnn} classifiers in industry. I do not research
large language models, but this is a sound basis for contributing to
deep-learning teaching while developing further expertise, emphasising
calibrated uncertainty, validation, model criticism, and the link between
modern predictive methods and statistical inference.
I am willing and able to contribute to in-person and hybrid teaching in the
Department's joint programme in Chongqing; my accepted invitation to lecture in
Beijing in 2026 reflects both that willingness and existing ties to the
Chinese research community.

\block{Service, community, and inclusive practice}

I serve both the Department and the wider research community: I have examined a
Master's thesis for the Department of Statistics, reviewed three manuscripts for
\emph{Physical Review D}, helped organise the April 2025 NZ Gravity Workshop at
Auckland, co-organised the NZ Gravity and Monash Astronomy journal clubs, and
help maintain widely used community research software including
\link{https://github.com/bilby-dev/bilby}{\software{bilby}},
\link{https://github.com/lscsoft/parallel_bilby}{\software{parallel\_bilby}},
and \link{https://github.com/TeamCOMPAS/COMPAS}{\software{COMPAS}}.

I value the Department's commitment to excellent teaching, student wellbeing,
and an inclusive environment, and would contribute through transparent
assessment expectations, accessible materials, and respectful mentoring. I
recognise the University's commitments under Te Tiriti o Waitangi and to
equity, diversity, and inclusion, and would continue learning how to give
effect to them across my work.

Thank you for considering my application. My CV is enclosed, and I would
welcome the opportunity to discuss my fit for the position.
