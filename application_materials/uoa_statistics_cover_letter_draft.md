# Draft cover letter: University of Auckland Statistics position

**[Date]**

Appointment Committee  
Te Kura Tatauranga | Department of Statistics  
Waipapa Taumata Rau | University of Auckland

Dear members of the Appointment Committee,

I am writing to apply for the permanent academic position in Statistics. I am
currently a Postdoctoral Research Fellow in the Department of Statistics at the
University of Auckland. My research sits at the intersection of Bayesian
statistics, computational methods, and gravitational-wave science. I would be
excited to contribute to the Department's undergraduate and postgraduate
teaching, research, supervision, and service as a continuing member of its
academic community.

My current research develops statistical methodology for difficult inference
problems in gravitational-wave astronomy. In particular, I work on Bayesian
spectral-density modelling, evidence estimation, variational inference, and
scalable computational tools for uncertainty quantification. This has recently
included Bayesian P-spline models for LISA noise, multivariate estimation of
spectral-density matrices, and methods for evidence estimation through density
approximation. These problems are motivated by gravitational-wave observations,
but the methods are broadly relevant to time-series analysis, non-parametric
modelling, Bayesian computation, and data science. At the 2025 AUT Mathematical
Modelling and Analytics Symposium, I presented this multivariate P-spline
methodology using EEG recordings and financial time series as well as
astrophysical data, demonstrating how the research connects with applications
beyond gravitational-wave science.

I am already embedded in the Department's gravitational-wave research
community and collaborate closely with Professor Renate Meyer, Matthew
Edwards, and Kate Lee. This existing network, together with my international
connections in the LIGO--Virgo--KAGRA and LISA communities, would allow me to
build an independent and collaborative research programme from the outset. I
would aim to develop a programme in Bayesian and computational statistics for
complex, high-dimensional, and time-dependent data; recruit and co-supervise
postgraduate students; and pursue external funding for methodological research
with scientific and wider data-science applications. I have already secured two
successful ADACS Merit Allocation awards for research-software development and
am Principal Investigator on a pending 2026 Dumont d'Urville NZ--France
application. I have also contributed to internal proposal development.

My teaching experience spans programming, algorithms, software engineering,
physics, and scientific data analysis. At Monash University I supported
FIT9136, Algorithms and Programming Foundations in Python, where I ran Python
workshops, developed automated grading, and contributed to the design of
assignments and examinations. In practice, I co-authored the assessments with
the teaching team for review by the course lecturer and graded the resulting
student work. For FIT3170, Software Engineering Practice, I supported three
years of project-based course delivery. Each year I mentored two agile teams of
approximately 20 students, monitored their application of the Scaled Agile
Framework, advised them on technical choices, graded their work, and recruited
guest lecturers and external clients for the course. I also delivered a guest
lecture for FIT3170.

My teaching is complemented by substantial research mentoring. At the
University of Auckland and through international collaborations, I have
supported PhD, MSc, and undergraduate researchers alongside their main
supervisors, working with them on variational inference, Bayesian spectral
estimation, neural surrogates, and normalising flows. I enjoy teaching the
connection between sound statistical reasoning, computation, and substantive
scientific questions. I have also designed and delivered tutorials on Bayesian
parameter estimation and parallel computing for NZ Gravity training events,
including the 2023 NZ Summer School on Gravitational Waves and the 2023 NZ
Gravity Workshop at Canterbury.

In the Department, my strongest immediate contributions would be to Data
Science Practice, Advanced Data Science Practice, Simulation and Monte Carlo
Methods, and Bayesian Inference. I would also be willing to contribute to large
Stage I service courses as part of a teaching team. Across these courses I
would bring an applied, project-led approach that connects statistical
reasoning with modern programming and real datasets.

The Department's interest in the statistical foundations of deep learning and
large language models aligns with my background in machine learning,
probabilistic inference, and scientific computing. I have worked with neural
surrogates and active machine learning in research, and previously worked with
NLP speech models and CNN classifiers. My contribution would emphasise
well-calibrated uncertainty, validation, model criticism, and the relationship
between modern predictive methods and statistical inference. I am also willing
and able to contribute to in-person teaching in the joint programme with
Southwestern University in Chongqing. I have been invited to lecture to
Master's and PhD students on Bayesian parameter estimation for compact binary
coalescences in LIGO and LISA at the 2026 International Training Workshop on
Frontiers from Quanta to Cosmos Physics in Beijing. I have accepted the
invitation and am awaiting my visa.

I contribute service to both the Department and the wider research community.
I have examined a thesis for the Department of Statistics, reviewed three
manuscripts for Physical Review D, helped organise the April 2025 NZ Gravity
Workshop at Auckland, and co-organised the NZ Gravity and Monash Astronomy
journal clubs. I also contribute to maintaining community research software,
including bilby, bilby_pipe, parallel_bilby, asimov, and COMPAS.

I value the Department's commitment to excellent teaching, student wellbeing,
and an inclusive academic environment. I would contribute through transparent
assessment expectations, accessible teaching materials, respectful mentoring,
and constructive collaboration with colleagues and students. I recognise the
University's commitments under Te Tiriti o Waitangi and to equity, diversity,
and inclusion, and would continue learning how to give effect to these
commitments in my teaching, supervision, research, and service.

Thank you for considering my application. I have enclosed my CV, including my
refereed publication record, teaching and supervision experience, and research
funding and service contributions. I would welcome the opportunity to discuss
my fit for the position.

Yours sincerely,

Avi Vajpeyi
